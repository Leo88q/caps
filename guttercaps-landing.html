Gutter City never dries

The lore behind every pack you open — why a bottle cap can carry a whole scene's reputation, and why that had to end up on-chain.

Gutter City never really dries out. Every storm drain in the old quarters backs up sooner or later, and when it does, the flood drags up everything the streets have swallowed — including bottle caps that sat close enough to a scene to soak up its charge. Skaters, writers, and DJs figured out you could seed a cap near a wall, a ramp, a rail yard, wait for the next flood, then dive for it once it surfaced charged. It turned into its own economy — and its own forgery problem, since a charge is easy to fake and hard to prove. That is the actual reason the whole chain of custody now lives on-chain instead of in somebody's notebook.

Marketplace

Listing a chip escrows it to the contract's PDA account. The buyer pays the seller directly — the studio only takes a fee with a hard-coded ceiling.

Rules & fairness

This section is deliberately boring and graffiti-free — it's about money, so precision matters more than mood.

  • The marketplace fee is fixed in the contract at 2.5% and can never exceed a 10% ceiling, no matter how the config is updated.
  • A dedicated oracle key signs every PvP result; the contract makes it mathematically impossible to send the payout to anyone but that battle's challenger or opponent.
  • Staking rewards follow "rarity rate × (1 + level / 5) × time"; rounding always favors the smallest possible payout.
  • The studio has an emergency pause flag — it blocks new pack purchases, listings, and staking during an incident, but never freezes what players already own.
  • Every meaningful action (pack opened, upgrade, sale, reward claim, battle resolution) writes an on-chain event that anyone can audit, not just the studio.

GUTTERCAPS is a collectible game with chance-based mechanics that use real funds. Secondary-market chip value is not guaranteed and can drop to zero. This is not an investment product or financial advice. 18+ recommended. Only play with funds you can afford to lose.

Why this is on-chain

Not because it's trendy — because some of the usual problems with collectible games only get solved this way.

The chip is actually yours

It sits in your wallet, not in the studio's database. If the app ever shuts down, the chip doesn't disappear — you can manage it directly through any Solana wallet.

The odds are checkable

Rarity weights are open program code, not a number in a hidden admin config. Every pack result is signed by a randomness oracle — not picked by a server after it sees your payment.

The market is open to everyone

You can sell a chip outside the game too — it reads in any Solana explorer and is compatible with external NFT marketplaces if you choose to list it there.

Wagers run on math, not trust

In PvP and staking, code moves the money, not a support team. You can verify where a payout will go before the fight, not after a dispute with an admin.
